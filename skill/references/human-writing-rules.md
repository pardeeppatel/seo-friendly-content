# Human-Writing & Anti-AI Guardrails

> **Developed by Pardeep Kumar** | **Powered by [ToolNPlay.com](https://ToolNPlay.com)**

Use this checklist during drafting and final editing so content reads like an experienced human subject-matter writer rather than automated prose.

---

## 1. Banned / High-Risk Robotic Vocabulary
Never cluster these words, and replace them wherever simpler, concrete wording works:

* **Overused Abstract & Editorial Clichés:** `delve`, `deep dive`, `tapestry` (abstract), `testament`, `landscape` (abstract), `interplay`, `intricate` / `intricacies`, `pivotal`, `crucial`, `underscore` (verb), `showcase` / `showcasing`, `foster` / `fostering`, `bolster` / `bolstered`, `garner`, `boasts` (meaning "has"), `meticulous` / `meticulously`, `vibrant`, `enduring`, `align with`, `enhance`, `emphasizing`, `highlighting`, `valuable` / `valuable insights`, `robust`, `Additionally` (opening a sentence).
* **Corporate & Synthetic Filler Words:** `multifaceted`, `leverage`, `prioritize`, `dependable`, `quietly`, `steady`, `universally`, `echoed`, `clearer` (used as canned framing).
* **Promotional Puffery:** `groundbreaking`, `renowned`, `exemplifies`, `commitment to`, `rich heritage`, `diverse array`, `nestled`, `in the heart of`, `seamless` / `seamlessly`.

> **Literal Exception:** Only use words like `landscape` (physical terrain) or `underscore` (an actual `_` character or music score) in their literal, physical sense.

## 2. Structural & Rhetorical Anti-Patterns

### A. Undue Significance & Legacy Puffery
* **Avoid:** *"marking a pivotal shift"*, *"serves as a reminder of"*, *"plays a crucial role in shaping the evolving landscape"*, *"leaving an indelible mark"*, *"deeply rooted in"*, *"generated debate about"*.
* **Write Instead:** State the concrete fact, number, or consequence directly.

### B. Trailing `-ing` Superficial Commentary
* **Avoid:** Tacking present-participle spin onto the end of sentences:
  - ❌ *"...helping businesses streamline workflows and underscoring the value of automation."*
  - ✅ *"Teams cut manual data entry by roughly four hours a week using automated triggers."*

### C. Avoidance of Simple Copulatives (`is` / `are` / `has`)
* **Avoid:** `serves as a`, `stands as a`, `functions as`, `operates as`, `represents a`, `holds the distinction of`, `features`, `offers`, `boasts`, `authored`, `utilized`, `relocated`, `passed away`.
* **Write Instead:** Plain, direct verbs: `is`, `are`, `was`, `has`, `wrote`, `used`, `moved`, `died`.

### D. Negative Parallelisms (Contrast Crutches)
* **Avoid:**
  - *"Not only X, but also Y"* / *"Not just X, but Y"*
  - *"It is not X — it's Y"* / *"No X, no Y, just Z"*
  - *"Y rather than X"* (especially as corrective framing)
* **Write Instead:** State what the subject actually does directly in one clean clause.

### E. Overuse of the "Rule of Three"
* **Avoid:** Defaulting to three adjectives (*"fast, reliable, and scalable"*) or forcing every section into 3-bullet summaries.
* **Write Instead:** Use 1–2 precise descriptors or vary list lengths naturally (2, 4, 5, or prose paragraphs).

### F. Vague Weasel Attributions
* **Avoid:** *"Experts argue"*, *"Industry reports suggest"*, *"Observers have noted"*, *"Some critics say"*, *"Several studies show"* (without naming and citing them).
* **Write Instead:** Name the exact organization, author, study, or tool—or omit the claim if unverified.

### G. Formulaic Sections & Conclusions
* **Avoid:**
  - `"Challenges and Future Prospects"` / `"Despite its strengths, X faces several challenges..."`
  - Formulaic `"X and Y"` headings like `"Awards and recognition"`
  - Closing headings or openers like `"In summary"`, `"In conclusion"`, `"Overall"` that merely repeat prior paragraphs.
* **Write Instead:** End with a specific decision framework, next step, or practical wrap-up tied to the reader's goal.

## 3. Formatting, Punctuation & Grounding Hygiene

1. **No Em-Dash Spam (and No Robotic Sentence Monotony):** Avoid spaced em-dashes (` — `) used for dramatic punch-ups. At the same time, vary sentence length naturally instead of locking into choppy 8–20 word monotony. Use parentheses, colons, semicolons, and varied clause lengths naturally.
2. **No Formatting Clichés:**
   - Never start headings or list items with emojis.
   - Never overuse `**bold**` inside normal body paragraphs.
   - Avoid endless `**Bold Label**: sentence` vertical lists; mix regular prose paragraphs, tables, and natural bullets.
3. **Respect Heading Casing & Hierarchy:** Keep one `H1` for the main title, logical `H2`/`H3`, and natural readable casing in minor subheadings.
4. **Zero Meta-Text or Hedging Disclaimers:**
   - Never leave *"Certainly!"*, *"Here is a..."*, *"I hope this helps"*, *"It is important to note/remember..."*, or *"While specific details are limited..."* inside the article.
5. **Zero Fake Citations or URL Artifacts:**
   - Never output `[INSERT_URL]`, `2026-XX-XX`, `example.com/...`, fake DOIs/ISBNs, or tracking query strings (`utm_source=`, `referrer=`).
6. **Human Cadence Signals:** Use specific concrete details (exact specs, real edge cases, candid trade-offs), occasional natural qualifiers (`perhaps`, `very`, `tends to`), and direct `is`/`has` statements.
